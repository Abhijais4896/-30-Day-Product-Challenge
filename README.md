# 🚀 30-Day Product Challenge

### **30 Days → 30 Products → 30 Real Problems → 30 Prototypes**

I'm spending 30 days exploring real products, finding meaningful customer problems, and turning those problems into product solutions.

This is a **build-in-public product challenge** focused on practicing product thinking through real-world research, problem framing, prototyping, and experimentation.

> **I don't start with the solution. I start with the problem.**

---

## 🎯 The Challenge

Every day, I'll pick a product from a different industry and investigate:

* 🔎 What are customers actually struggling with?
* 🔁 Is the problem recurring?
* ⚠️ How severe is the pain?
* 🛠️ What workarounds are users using?
* 🥊 How are competitors handling it?
* 💡 Is there a genuine product opportunity?
* 🧪 Can I test a possible solution?

Then I'll take one problem through:

```text
Research
   ↓
Problem Framing
   ↓
Hypothesis
   ↓
Solution
   ↓
Prototype
   ↓
Metrics
   ↓
Experiment
   ↓
Learning
```

## 🧠 What I'm Practicing

This challenge is not just about creating UI.

I'm practicing the complete product-thinking process:

### 🔍 Customer Research

Finding real customer pain points through public feedback, reviews, communities, forums, and other available signals.

### 🎯 Problem Discovery

Separating a genuine product problem from a simple complaint or isolated incident.

### 🧩 Problem Framing

Understanding who experiences the problem, when it happens, why it matters, and what users currently do about it.

### 💡 Product Thinking

Exploring possible solutions while considering user value, business impact, feasibility, and trade-offs.

### 🎨 Prototyping

Turning ideas into practical, clickable prototypes rather than just static screens.

### 📊 Metrics

Defining how we could measure whether the proposed solution actually works.

### 🧪 Experimentation

Thinking about how the solution could be tested before investing heavily in it.

## 📈 Challenge Principles

### 1. Customer first

I won't invent a problem and then search for evidence to support it.

### 2. Evidence over assumptions

One complaint isn't automatically a product problem.

I'll look for multiple signals wherever possible.

### 3. Problem before solution

The goal isn't to brainstorm features.

It's to understand the underlying problem first.

### 4. Prototype over theory

Whenever practical, I'll build something people can actually interact with.

### 5. Metrics matter

A solution isn't successful just because it looks good.

I'll define what success could actually look like.

### 6. Share the messy parts

Not every hypothesis will be correct.

Not every solution will be good.

That's part of the challenge.
